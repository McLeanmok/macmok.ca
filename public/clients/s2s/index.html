01 · Executive summary

A meaningful experience, clearly explained

Shoulder 2 Shoulder creates “Snow Therapy” camp experiences that bring injured Canadian Armed Forces Veterans, RCMP members, and their support persons together for respite, connection, challenge, and time in the mountains.

Core offer
Snow Therapy camps

Shared mountain experiences delivered with CADS, alpine resorts, volunteers, and local partners. The site says participants and their support person can take part without financial strain.

Primary audience
Potential participants and support people

Veterans and RCMP members are central in the mission statement. A recent camp announcement also names First Responders; confirm whether this is an eligibility expansion.

Visible actions
Apply · Donate · Connect

Applications appear to be the participant journey’s main action; donations support camp delivery; Connect provides a general contact route.

Near-term context
2027 camp season

The site lists six sessions across five mountain destinations. A September 2026 post says registration opened October 1, 2026; current availability should be confirmed.

Strategic opportunity: make it immediately clear who can apply, what the camp experience includes, how support persons participate, what it costs, and what happens after applying—while giving donors and partners their own clear paths.

This is a desk-research snapshot, not a completed stakeholder interview or approved strategy. Statements below are distinguished as published facts, observations, or hypotheses.

02 · Organization & offer

What the organization does

The public site describes an opportunity to step away from daily pressures, find respite, reconnect with nature, and challenge oneself through snow sports. It emphasizes friendship, shared experience, confidence, renewed possibility, and not feeling alone.

How the program is presented

  • Program language: “Snow Therapy” healing camps for injured Veterans, RCMP members, and a support person.
  • Experience: snow sports and time in mountain settings, supported by volunteers and local partners.
  • Financial access: the site says the opportunity is provided without financial strain; confirm exactly which costs are covered and any participant responsibilities.
  • Delivery network: a stated partnership with CADS and alpine resorts across Canada.
  • Funding: donations are solicited through Zeffy. The homepage states 100% of donations go to support a CF Veteran or RCMP member and their support person; confirm the precise wording and accounting basis before carrying it into new copy.

03 · Audience & decision journey

People arrive with different needs

The website speaks to participants, support people, donors, and community partners. These audiences need distinct information and next steps.

AudienceLikely question to answerBest next step
Veterans / RCMP membersAm I eligible? What is the experience like? Is this right for me?Review eligibility and apply
Support person / caregiverCan I attend too? What will I be expected to do? What support is available?Understand the shared experience and application
DonorsWhat does my gift make possible? How is it used?Donate and see impact
Partners / volunteersHow can our organization or skills contribute?Connect with the team

Audience insight (hypothesis to validate)

Someone considering a camp may be interested but uncertain about eligibility, logistics, ability level, privacy, or whether they will feel comfortable. The website should reduce uncertainty with candid practical detail and a welcoming, non-pressuring tone. These are research prompts—not confirmed participant quotes or findings.

Potential before → after story

Before: carrying day-to-day pressure and feeling disconnected or unsure about trying something new.
After: having shared time outdoors, a chance to challenge oneself, and new connection and confidence to carry forward.

This transformation reflects the organization’s published mission language. Do not promise clinical or therapeutic outcomes unless the organization can substantiate and approve them.

Likely conversion journeys

  1. Participant discovers the program → understands eligibility and camp details → submits an application → receives confirmation and next-step communication.
  2. Support person evaluates participation → understands role and logistics → joins the participant’s application or contacts the team.
  3. Donor learns what camps make possible → sees credible impact and partner proof → donates.
  4. Potential partner or volunteer learns how the program works → sees current needs → connects with the organization.

05 · Brand, positioning & opportunity

What feels distinctive—and what to clarify

Publicly expressed brand qualities

WelcomingResilientSupportiveOutdoors-orientedCommunity-led

Voice is warm, hopeful, direct, and grounded in shared experience. The “Shoulder to Shoulder. On the mountain. Together.” line captures companionship and place. Balance inspirational language with plain, practical answers.

Working positioning hypothesis

For injured Veterans and RCMP members—and the people who support them—Shoulder 2 Shoulder offers welcoming snow-sport camp experiences that create room for respite, connection, and renewed possibility, with a companion by their side.

Working hypothesis derived from public copy; needs client approval, audience validation, and careful review of who is eligible.

Potential differentiators to substantiate

  • The participant attends with a support person or caregiver.
  • Costs are structured to avoid financial strain for participants.
  • Snow-sport experiences are enabled by a cross-country network of CADS, resorts, volunteers, and donors.

Market and evidence gaps

  • No competitor or alternative-program research was supplied or verified. Do not claim uniqueness until comparable programs and the “do nothing” alternative are understood.
  • Collect approved participant stories, testimonials, program outcomes, camp photos, and partner endorsements where consent permits.
  • Clarify what “Snow Therapy” means operationally; distinguish the program’s supportive intent from regulated clinical care or guaranteed health outcomes.

Redesign priorities suggested by this research

  1. Put the audience, eligibility, and core promise in the first screen.
  2. Make the participant pathway and application status unmistakable.
  3. Explain the camp experience, support-person role, logistics, costs, and what happens after applying.
  4. Give donors a concise, credible impact story and a clear donation action.
  5. Show real people and partner credibility with permissions, captions, and accessible alternatives.
  6. Keep seasonal schedules, deadlines, and capacity easy to update and verify.
06 · Client discovery

Questions to complete the brief

These are the remaining decisions that need the client’s knowledge. A short interview will turn this research snapshot into an agreed strategy.

  1. What is the single most important result of the new website?More eligible applications, donations, partner/volunteer leads, awareness—or a ranked combination?
  2. What's the most common objection that participants have when applying? Do they feel like this program is for "special" people? Do they feel that they don't have the skills to participate? Are they insecure that the program will suit their needs?
  3. Who is eligible to apply today?Veterans, RCMP members, First Responders, and which service statuses, regions, or injury criteria? Is a support person required or optional?
  4. What exactly is included in a camp?Accommodation, meals, equipment, instruction, lift tickets, travel, accessibility support, and any participant costs or requirements?
  5. How does the application and selection process work?Where does the form go, what happens after submission, how are places allocated, when are decisions made, and what happens if a camp is full?
  6. What is the current 2027 status?Are applications open? Which locations have space or waitlists? Is the published schedule final?
  7. What real participant language and evidence may we use?Approved quotes, testimonials, outcomes, return-participant stories, photos, partner endorsements, and consent constraints?
  8. What should a donor understand about impact?What does a typical gift fund? Is “100% of donations go to support a participant and support person” exact, current, and approved wording?
  9. What makes the organization meaningfully different?What alternatives do applicants consider, and why do participants, caregivers, and partners choose Shoulder 2 Shoulder?
  10. What are the brand and operational constraints?Who approves voice/design, what existing identity assets must remain, what privacy/accessibility/legal requirements apply, and who maintains seasonal content?
  11. Which pages or capabilities are in scope?Keep or replace the current site structure? Any application integrations, donor tools, volunteer/partner information, FAQs, galleries, or account needs?
  12. What's the most sensible organization for the website? Do you feel we need multiple pages - why? How can we make the website as easy as possible to use and navigate?
  13. What's the primary access method? How will users acess the website? On the computer? Phone? Tablet? Do they use a screen reader Or Text-to-Speech software? Do the users have any acessabillity requirements that need to be accounted for??
  14. Are there any other sites in this niche? In your opinion, what are they doing well? Where do they fall short? Please send over 3-5links
Once these answers are confirmed, update the project brief and review it with the client before moving into messaging, page structure, wireframes, or visual design.
